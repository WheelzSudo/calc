How Freelancers Calculate Their True Hourly Rate

Transitioning from a traditional salary to freelancing requires switching from a net income mindset to a gross revenue requirement mindset. When working as an employee, your employer pays taxes, health insurance, equipment, and paid time off. As a freelancer, your hourly rate must account for these overheads directly.

The Freelance Pricing Formula

To establish your minimum viable hourly rate, use the standard baseline formula:

Gross Revenue Needed = (Target Salary + Business Expenses) ÷ (1 - Tax Rate) × (1 + Profit Margin)

Minimum Hourly Rate = Gross Revenue Needed ÷ Total Annual Billable Hours

Billable vs. Non-Billable Hours Benchmarks

A common mistake among new contractors is assuming a 40-hour work week equals 40 billable hours. In reality, independent professionals spend 30% to 50% of their time on unbillable administration, client prospecting, and administrative work.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate my freelance hourly rate?

Combine your desired net take-home pay and annual expenses, adjust upward for self-employment tax obligations, add a 10%-20% profit reserve, and divide by total billable hours per year.

What is the difference between hourly rates and project-based pricing?

Hourly rates bill for actual time spent, protecting you during scope creep. Project-based pricing charges a fixed price derived from estimated billable hours multiplied by your internal hourly rate, rewarding efficiency and expertise.

Should I charge an hourly rate or a day rate?

Day rates are typically calculated by multiplying your hourly rate by 8 hours. Day rates work best for short-term consulting, workshops, or on-site client sprints where full-day dedication is required.
